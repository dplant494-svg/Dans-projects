**Topics I would bring to days 3 and 4:**

*SACRED review*
- What SACRED is today: the report pipeline (tool, intake, SharePoint, scanner, the dashboards on the
  sacred share), the Copilot agent over every posted report (Ask SACRED AI), the precharge, CBM to OEM,
  AAB and Help Centre loops, all on one pattern: a tool joins by posting a file, nothing else changes.
- Revision control as it works now: every tool carries a REV badge, every change is announced in a
  written handoff before it ships, and every key the tools post is written down against what the
  dashboard does with it. Where that is enough and where a formal document-control step is still
  needed.
- Lessons-learned capability: the Help Centre trail, the AAB acknowledgements with evidence, the
  problems feed on the Errors button. What is captured today and what is not.
- Usability: what the rigs actually click, from the class on days 1 and 2, straight into the action
  list.

*CBM lessons learned*
- Riser Adapter could not be photographed or graded for five weeks and nobody noticed until a rig said
  so. What the check is now, and why announcing keys before they ship matters.
- The positional key failure: 38 of 65 CBM grade keys silently changed meaning when the template was
  rebuilt. How the id-based keys and the key map fixed it, and the rule for the future.
- NOV's criteria on the live path: 85 of 110 graded tasks carry NOV's own five-level wording; the
  rest use the universal guide. The mapping work still open, and the double handling it removes.
- The 40 MB ceiling and evidence: photographs compressed at source, test records attached from
  SSORT 152, never degraded to fit a limit.
- The OEM copy to NOV: what they receive, what they cannot yet (the attachments), and the
  double handling between our report and their review.
- Maximo: the daily item export already feeds SPARC and the COC tracker; where it lands is still the
  open question, and it decides how much of the job-plan alignment is free.

*Efficiency forum*
- Every notification carries a link to the dashboard, so the report is read once, where it lives.
- SSORT tells a crew "posted" when the post failed; WCGRRT does not. One revision, after the class.
- The precharge loop as the template: request to issued sheet to notification to record, nobody
  retypes anything. The same template for the SSCE release flow and the failure notification.
- What is still on paper or in email that could be a post.

*For the standards and TOMs sessions* I have no content of my own, but the reporting tools carry
NOV's task wording and the pre-deployment checklist as written, so whatever the group decides on
structure has a place to land.
