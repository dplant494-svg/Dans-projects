The story

Former rig worker turned coach.

I'm Dan Plant. Nearly twenty years in oil and gas, most of it as a subsea engineer on drilling rigs: West Africa, the Gulf, Brazil. Twenty-eight days on, twenty-eight off. I've trained for twenty-five years and never once had the consistency. The rotation looked like a built-in detox. It was the opposite.

I drank from a young age, and for a long time it did what I needed it to. Then it didn't. On 2 April 2025 I stopped. I didn't do it on my own: I rang a helpline, walked into a room full of people who'd been where I was, and did what I was told. The programme and the people in it saved my life. I haven't drunk since.

On 2 July 2025, three months sober, I walked back into a gym, this time with a coach: Shaun Joseph Tavenier, IFBB pro and former Mr Olympia competitor. His system, his ethos, and for the first time in twenty-five years, my consistency. Do what you're told, follow the process, and the results come. I took a photo on day one and filmed everything after it, because I didn't trust myself to remember what it had been like. I was 72 pounds heavier than I am now.

I measure it by how I feel, what I learn and what I put into action, not just the number on the counter. I'm training as a personal trainer so I can coach this properly, not just tell you what worked for me. What I coach is the process I was given: the same ethos, applied to your gym, your rig or your hotel room. Roughneck Fitness is for the bloke I was in that photo, drink in hand. I know how to get you out, because someone got me out.

— Coach Dan Plant. Former rig worker. Sober since 2 April 2025.

Who it's for

Wherever you train.

Most plans assume a full gym and a routine. Real life has night shifts, travel, hotels, and weeks where the nearest kit is a pair of dumbbells. Every plan here has a version for that, and the same diet guide runs through all three.

Full gym

Build properly

Four sessions a week on a full rack, cables and machines. Progressive overload written in, so every week has a target. The plan that built the after photos.

Rig or site gym

Train with what's there

A container gym with a rack, a few dumbbells and a cable stack. Twelve-hour shifts accounted for. Canteen and galley food ordered the right way.

Hotel room

No excuses on the road

Bodyweight, a band and whatever dumbbells the hotel gym has. Forty minutes, no kit that won't fit in a carry-on. Restaurant and room-service food that doesn't wreck the week.

How it works

Do what you're told. Follow the process.

That's the whole secret, and it's the one I was given. Here's what it looks like.

  1. Apply

    Two minutes. Where you train, what kit you've got, your goal, and whether drink is part of the picture. No judgement either way.

  2. Call

    Twenty minutes on the phone. If it's not a fit I'll say so and point you somewhere better.

  3. Plan

    Your programme lands in the app before Monday, built for the gym you'll actually be in, with the diet guide alongside it.

  4. Report

    Weekly check-in: weight, photos, sessions done, how the weekend went. Adjust, repeat, next week.
