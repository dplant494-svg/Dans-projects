The espeto

Six sardines, one cane, and the embers of a boat.

In Málaga they say sardines are for the months without an R. From May to August the sardine is fat, and the espeto is at its best.

The espeto was born on the beaches of El Palo more than a century ago: fishermen threaded sardines onto a cane, pushed it into the sand beside the embers and let the heat, not the flame, do the work. We do it the same way, in an old boat filled with sand, over olive wood, with coarse salt. Nothing else.

The rest of the menu comes off the same coals and from Marbella's fish market: whatever came in that day.

The boat, the cane and the embers. Just as in El Palo a hundred years ago.

A day at Brasa y Sal

From morning coffee to the last of the embers.

10:00

Breakfast

Coffee, freshly squeezed orange juice and toast with tomato, olive oil and ham. Facing the sea, no hurry.

13:30

Espetos and vermouth

The boat is lit at one. First espeto on the table at half past. Set lunch on weekdays.

20:45

Sunset

Golden hour moves every week; we chalk it up on the board. A glass of dry muscat and prawns off the plancha.

22:00

Dinner over the coals

Beef chop, presa, octopus. The olive-wood embers hold until the last table. Kitchen until half past eleven.
