Seadrill Technical Services · Planning · Argo Navis (formerly DL-1 / BOP Schedule Builder)

Argo Navis Field Guide

Everything you need to take a BOP end-of-well maintenance project from a pile of Maximo PMs to a sequenced schedule on the Planning Dashboard. No prior knowledge assumed. Work through it in order the first time; after that you'll only need Step 5 and the weekly rhythm.

What this thing actually does

It builds the schedule, then tracks it

Everything else in the reporting family reports against a schedule someone already built by hand in P6. This is the one tool that builds the schedule itself — it reads the rig's outstanding PMs, works out what order the work has to happen in, stacks up the hours, and gives you dates. Then you report progress against it every cycle and it pushes to the dashboard.

Three things go in, one thing comes out:

Goes inWhat it gives the tool
WO.csv
from \\SEADRILL23339\Well Control Data		Every open Well Control work order across the fleet, refreshed 06:00 Houston daily — the raw scope
Job Plans and Durations.xlsmHow many hours each job plan takes
Job Plan Branch  Sequence Map.xlsxThe rules: which branch each job belongs to, and in what order

Out comes a ..._Report.json file that lands on the BOP Fleet Planning Dashboard, plus two printable documents — a Scope Freeze pack and a Planning Report.

Why the third file matters most

The Branch & Sequence Map is the tool's brain. It's a spreadsheet Dan Plant maintains by hand, and it decides whether a job is Lower BOP work, LMRP work, Controls, Offline Ancillary or Testing — and what has to finish before it can start. If a schedule comes out looking wrong, that workbook is usually the reason, not the tool.

Before you touch anything

Getting it open

You need Chrome or Edge on a desktop or laptop. Not an iPad — this is a wide-screen tool and always has been.

Start the little local server first, then open the tool in your browser:

powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File "Scripts/Serve-Static.ps1" -Root "REV 13" -Port 8966

Leave that window alone — it needs to stay running while you work. Then go to http://localhost:8966 in your browser. Close the window when you're finished for the day.

Why not just double-click the file

You can, and the tool will open. But two of the best features stop working: it can't remember your data folder (so you'd pick all three files by hand, every single time), and Sync downloads to your Downloads folder instead of saving straight to the shared reporting folder. Browsers only allow folder access over a proper web address, which is what the server provides.

First-time setup, once only

On your very first run you'll click three "pick a folder" buttons — two in Step 1 (the WO feed share and the rules folder), and one on your first Sync for the shared reporting folder. The tool remembers all three permanently. You never do it again.

The five steps

Building a schedule from scratch

The tabs across the top run left to right. You genuinely do need to do them in order the first time, because each one feeds the next.

1

Import Data

Not your first report on this job?

Then skip this whole page. Hit Continue Existing Schedule at the top, pick the JSON file from your last Sync, and it drops you straight into Step 5 with everything as you left it. Full explanation in The weekly rhythm below.

Why two folders and not one

The WO feed is machine-written to a server share. The other two are hand-maintained — Dan Plant edits the Branch Map — so they stay where they're edited. Copying them next to the feed would create a second copy that quietly goes out of date, which is worse than clicking one extra button once.

You should end up with three green ticks and a summary confirming all three inputs are loaded. If Next: Commencement Setup stays greyed out, the panel underneath tells you exactly which input is still missing and which button provides it.

After the first time it does this automatically whenever you open the tool. If someone's refreshed a spreadsheet, click Reload Everything.

Fleet backlog — the reason importing comes first

Once the feed loads you get two things. First a scope picker — the SFI groups in the feed, with a count against each. All are selected by default; untick any you aren't planning. Work is scoped by SFI group rather than by maintenance discipline, so equipment is planned whoever maintains it: 93 of the BOP control-system work orders in SFI 332 are owned by Electrical, and all 132 MPD work orders sit in SFI 339 — all of it comes through just the same.

Then a table of every rig's outstanding backlog in that scope: how many open work orders each is carrying, and how much of it the Branch Map can actually sequence.

That's the whole point of loading data before setting the campaign up — you can see where the work actually is, and whether a rig's schedule will be meaningful, before committing to a rig and a date. A rig showing a low "Sequenceable" percentage will produce a thin schedule whatever dates you give it.

2

Commencement Setup

Now tell it what job this is. Six fields:

Commencement Date
The moment the BOP lands on the beams. Every date in the schedule counts forward from here, so get it right.
Rig
Each rig shows how many open work orders it has in today's feed. A rig with no Maximo site mapped, or none of its work in the feed, is greyed out and says which.
BOP
BOP1 or BOP2. This filters the scope — you get that stack and its control pods plus shared rig equipment, and nothing belonging to the other stack.
Type of Pull
BWM (between-well maintenance), HOP, or Unplanned.
BOP Deployment Date + days
Optional but recommended. When the BOP goes back subsea. It sets the scope window: only Maximo work orders due up to the deployment date plus 21 days (change the days if you need to) come into the campaign, instead of everything due in the next three months. That gives a shorter, more realistic schedule — on a West Vela BOP1 test, deployment 15 days after commencement cut the scope from 143 to 108 work orders and from 918 to 719 hours. It also gives Total Float — how much slack you have before the BOP is wanted back.
Schedule Name
Fills itself in, e.g. 20260901_WAU_BWM_BOP1_Schedule. Read-only, and that's deliberate — the dashboard identifies your project by this name.

Under the fields you'll see a coverage note that updates as you change rig, BOP or date. It tells you how many work orders are in the feed for that rig, how many belong to the BOP you picked, and how many will actually appear in Step 3 — so the next page never surprises you with a different number.

Not every work order comes through. It takes:

  • work for your rig only (matched on its Maximo Site ID) and your BOP (or shared rig equipment), that is due — or already overdue — by the deployment date + 21 days when you have set one, or otherwise within 3 months and 1 week of commencement, and
  • every BWM-tagged work order, whatever its due date — because those are always done at a BWM.
3

Scope Challenge

This is the part that needs your judgement rather than the computer's. Every PM inside the scope window arrives ticked Execute. Untick anything you're deferring to a later window. That's the whole scope challenge: what's genuinely getting done this time.

The milling question — every plan

Above the task form: "Was any milling conducted in the previous well or during the current campaign?" Answer it before building the schedule (the tool stops you until you do). Yes means milling debris may have reached the annular and ram packers: press Prefill annular inspection task, set its hours, add it, and note what was milled — it is carried to the After Action Review.

Task types: Work, MOC, AAB — and one task in every plan

Add Task now has a Type. A modification is an MOC task with its MOC number; an AAB to be conducted is an AAB task with its AAB number. MoCs… lists the Synergi MoCs raised on this rig (from 90 days before commencement) — add each real modification with one click. These feed pre-deployment lines B-04 and B-17.

Every new plan also carries SSS-007 BOP handling equipment function test (DIR-37-0165), an Offline Ancillary task marked EVERY PLAN that can't be removed. When it reaches 100% it answers pre-deployment line B-19.

"Unassigned", "not on the job plan lists", and the Job plan gaps report

Unassigned means the work order's job plan is not on the Branch & Sequence Map, so the tool has no branch, parallel group or order for it and cannot sequence it with Lower BOP, LMRP or Controls. If it is also missing from Job Plans and Durations it shows 0 h.

not on the job plan lists means the job plan is on none of the three Maximo job plan list exports. That is a gap in the exports, not proof the plan is inactive: the Electrical list holds only 23 plans, so a live plan such as 0900-EE90061 (13 open WOs) is flagged.

Job plan gaps (Step 1 and the toolbar here) lists every job plan used by open work orders that is missing from the Branch Map, Durations, the saved job plans, the Maximo lists or the checklist file, most work orders first, with who fixes each and a CSV download. Tick this rig only to narrow it.

Excluded on Branch Map marks a job plan Dan has deliberately taken out of the BWM (his Excluded sheet: in-service subsea tests such as the 7-day subsea function test, riser gantry crane work). Its work orders arrive deferred; tick Execute only if this campaign really does it. A blue Maximo flag on a duration means the job plan is not in Job Plans and Durations, so the hours are the Maximo job plan's own figure — confirm it or type over it.

A red "feed is N days old" banner means WO.csv has not refreshed — the scope and due dates are out of date until it does.

Every active PM is accounted for

Boxes at the top give the count against the scope window: Active in Maximo (every open PM for this rig and BOP in your SFI groups), Captured by the window (how many to execute, how many deferred), Not captured (due after the window) and Added by hand.

The PMs that are not captured are listed below the scope table (click the orange bar to open it), soonest first, with how many days past the window each one falls. Tick Execute on any of them to bring it into this campaign — it moves into the scope marked ADDED · OUTSIDE WINDOW. Untick it again and it goes back to the list (it is not counted as a deferral, because it was never due in this window). Execute All never pulls the outside list in; those are chosen one at a time.

The SFI / Location column

Each row shows its SFI group with the full Maximo location underneath — 331.LMRP1.700, 332.BCS1.120, 339.MPD1.300. That's the reference a technician uses to find the equipment, so it's shown in full rather than shortened.

An orange discipline label underneath (MECHANICAL, ELECTRICAL, MPD) means the work is maintained outside Well Control. It's still in your scope — SFI decides that — but it tells you whose people you'll need.

Group by Branch or SFI code

The Group by buttons switch how both the Scope Challenge and the Step 4 schedule are organised. Each group heading carries its own counts and hours.

Branch (the default) shows the work the way it will actually be executed — Lower BOP, LMRP, Controls and so on, which is how the engine sequences it.

SFI code splits everything by equipment group instead, so you can review all the riser work together, or check exactly what's in scope for 339. Discipline groups it by who does the work. Switching is purely a change of view — it never alters the schedule or the dates.

Discipline — WCE, MPD, Electrical

The Discipline chips under the toolbar show just one discipline's work orders, with a count on each chip. The discipline comes from the work order's owner group in Maximo — who Maximo has assigned to do it.

Use it to hand each discipline its own list: pick MPD, then print the Scope Freeze pack and you get the MPD worklist, with the view named in its header. The same chips appear on the Schedule and Execute pages.

It only changes what is listed. The schedule, the crew levelling and the finish date always include every discipline — they work the same campaign.

NC column — Maximo Non-Conformity

If a work order won't be completed before its Maximo due date — because you are deferring it, or because the schedule finishes it late — tick NC and type the Maximo NC reference into the box that appears.

The schedule can't be built until every ticked NC has its reference. Next: Build Schedule, and the Schedule and Execute tabs, stop and take you to the first one missing. A red banner above the table lists them.

An orange NC? next to the box is only a prompt: the dates suggest an NC may be needed (already overdue, deferred, or planned to finish after the due date). You decide.

Cert column — status straight from the WCE CoC Tracker

With the CoC Tracker connected (Step 1, folder 3), each work order whose asset is in the tracker shows exactly the status the tracker shows for it, in the tracker's own colours: Expired, Due within 6 months, Due within 1 year, Due within 18 months, Valid or No date on file (short labels in the column: ≤ 6 mo, ≤ 1 yr and so on). DL-1 reads the tracker's own register and applies its own rules, so the counts agree with the tracker's sidebar.

Click the dot for part number, serial number, certificate number and the eDOCS link. Click ↗ to open that asset in the CoC Tracker. It is matched on the Maximo asset number; where an asset has several certified parts, the worst one sets the dot. The note above the table says how old the tracker data is.

Job Plan link, lessons and references

Click any Job Plan number to open the saved Maximo job plan — its steps, labour and materials. This works on every page and in the printed pack.

The Lessons & references column counts the Synergi events, alerts, HSE events and MoCs, and the eDOCS TB / TR / TP / IR / AAB documents, held against that Maximo location. Click a number to read them. Counts are fleet-wide (all rigs); the drill-down can narrow to your rig.

Exact and SFI-level are two separate slices, not two versions of one number. Exact counts records at this work order's location and everything below it. SFI-level counts records at the locations above it plus SFI-wide records with no location. They never overlap — add the two rows for the full total. A work order on a whole stack (e.g. 331.BOP) has a large Exact count because everything on the stack sits below it.

Matched to the Description. The counts only include records that name the work order's own equipment — the equipment words in its Description (e.g. ANNULAR, SHEAR RAM, ACCUMULATOR, with common abbreviations such as BSR, MUX or C&K understood). Exact needs one such word in the record; SFI-level, a wider net, needs it in the record's title (or two words). Very common words only count together with another. The list opens with the matched words highlighted, and Show all n records (unfiltered) brings back everything under the location if you want it.

NOV AX procedures — the correct revision, one click

Save a copy of the Well Control document register (Seadrill_WellControl_AllVessels_Upload.xlsx) into the Job Plans folder and it loads with the job plans; or use Load register. Every NOV AX procedure a job plan calls out — however it is written (AX050091, AX-061272, AX080453-45 Rev A) — becomes a link, AX080453 · Rev C ↗, that opens the register's eDOCS document, which is always the current version.

An AX ×n chip in the Job Plan cell lists the procedures for that job; NOV procedures on the toolbar lists them for the whole executed scope, and the Scope Freeze pack prints the same table for pre-job preparation.

Orange flags mean the job plan text is out of step with the register: JP cites Rev A · current Rev C, not in register, or register eDOCS 11251572 beside an eDOCS number the job plan quotes that isn't the register's. Use the linked register document and raise the job plan correction in Maximo.

Spare parts

Where a Maximo job plan carries a Materials list, a Parts n chip shows it (item number, description, quantity, storeroom). Spare parts on the toolbar adds them up for the executed scope — quantity × the number of work orders using that job plan — with a Download CSV for ordering, and the Scope Freeze pack prints it. The note says how many job plans have parts on file; the list grows as Materials are added to the job plans in Maximo.

The Unit column — which BOP each job belongs to

BOP1 or BOP2 means the job is on that stack or its control pods, and only appears when you have selected that BOP in Step 2.

SHARED means rig equipment serving both — riser, diverter, choke & kill, surface controls. Shared work appears in either campaign, which is why a BOP1 plan legitimately contains riser jobs.

A note above the table gives the split, so you can see the isolation is working.

You can also fix things inline:

  • Duration — overtype it if you know the job plan's figure is wrong.
  • Techs — how many technicians the job occupies. This drives the crew levelling in Step 4, so it's worth getting roughly right on the big jobs. A tinted box means there's no figure on file and it's assuming 1.
  • Parallel Group — change it to move a job into a different bundle of concurrent work.
  • Add Task — for work with no PM behind it at all (greasing, a one-off inspection). Give it a name, hours and a branch.
The SSCE column — 60-month tasks only

60-month work is the heavy overhaul scope: annular BOP field overhauls, diverter housing overhauls, pipe ram block inspections. If a certified SSCE asset will be available, the job becomes a change-out rather than an in-place overhaul — much quicker.

Tick SSCE on those rows and the duration switches to the change-out figure held against that job plan. Un-tick it and the original duration comes straight back. The banner tells you how many hours you've saved across the job.

The column shows — on anything that isn't a 60-month task, so most rows stay quiet. You'll typically see only a handful per campaign, which is right: 60-month work is five-yearly, so little of it is ever due at once.

Two things to know about SSCE

"n/a" means no figure is on record yet. Ticking the box then leaves the duration alone rather than guessing a saving. Either type the change-out hours into the Duration column, or get the job plan's figure added to the Branch Map's SSCE Change-Out Hours column.

A red ▲ means the change-out figure is longer than the overhaul it replaces. That's either a wrong figure or a job not worth changing out — the tool applies what the data says but flags it, because it would otherwise quietly push your finish date out.

Is the SSCE actually approved? When the SSCE requests data is loaded, the cell shows the request raised for that work order's asset through the COC dashboard: APPROVED, REQUESTED (waiting on the WCE SSCE Requests dashboard owner), DENIED, or NO REQUEST. Hover for who approved it and when. A ticked change-out without an approved request is a scope risk — the Scope Freeze pack lists the status beside each one.

Raising an SSCE application

When the CoC Tracker holds SSCE spares for a work order's asset (Shared Capital items with the same OEM part number), the SSCE cell shows e.g. 3 SSCE spares · 2 avail. ›. Click it to see each spare — asset, serial, its own certification status, and whether it's Available or Allocated to another rig.

Request SSCE in the CoC Tracker opens the tracker on that asset. Click the component, then Request beside the spare you want: the tracker's own SSCE Application Form opens pre-filled with the asset and the spare, and posts it to Central Spares for approval on the WCE SSCE Requests dashboard. The application is raised in one place only — the tracker — so there is never a second copy to reconcile.

4

Schedule

Set four durations for the fixed rig activities the PMs don't cover — Rack Back, Split BOP/LMRP, Stack Up and Drift Test — then decide the one question that changes the shape of the whole job:

Resources available to run BOP and LMRP simultaneously?

Ticked — you have separate crews, so Lower BOP and LMRP work run side by side. Shorter job.

Unticked — one crew does both, so LMRP can't start until Lower BOP is finished. Longer job.

Controls (pods) always runs alongside either way — that's a different discipline and a different crew.

Crew available per discipline

Enter how many technicians each discipline actually has — Lower BOP, LMRP, Controls, Offline Ancillary, Testing. The schedule then queues work behind the crew instead of assuming everything can run at once.

This matters more than it sounds. Left blank, the tool will cheerfully schedule 18 or more jobs simultaneously, which is a plan no rig can execute. Put real numbers in and the dates become achievable.

Leave a field blank (or 0) to mean unlimited — sensible for Offline Ancillary if that's 3rd-party or ship's crew. Blank everywhere reproduces exactly how earlier revisions behaved.

Hit Generate Schedule. The tool stacks hours forward continuously — 24/7, no shift gaps, because rig ops run back-to-back 12-hour shifts. The shape it builds:

BOP on beams → Rack Back → Split → Lower BOP LMRP Controls Offline Ancillary → Stack Up → Testing → Drift Test

The dashed branches run concurrently. Stack Up waits for Lower BOP, LMRP and Controls to all finish. Offline Ancillary runs alongside but doesn't hold Stack Up up — that's the point of it.

One clever bit worth knowing: if several PMs share the same physical piece of kit — say a BWM, a 3-monthly and a 6-monthly all on the same ram — the tool treats them as one visit. They start together and the group finishes when the longest one does, rather than adding all three durations together.

The schedule table now carries three extra columns worth understanding:

ColumnWhat it tells you
TechsTechnicians the job occupies. 1 unless the Branch Map's Techs Required column says otherwise.
Float (h)Spare time before that task starts delaying the gate after it. 0 means critical — the row is tinted and tagged. A task with 40h float can slip two days without hurting you; a critical one can't slip at all.
QueuedHours the task was pushed back purely because the crew was busy. Nothing here means the crew size isn't constraining that job.

Above the table, a per-discipline summary shows crew set against peak actually used — the quickest way to see whether a discipline is the bottleneck, and whether one more pair of hands would pull the finish in.

Float tells you about sequence, not about crew

Float answers "can this slip without moving the end date?". It does not mean the task is free to start earlier — the crew may be fully committed. That's what the Queued column is for. The two are kept separate deliberately, because treating queued time as float would tell you that you have freedom you haven't got.

"Planned to finish after their Maximo due date"

If this banner appears, some work you are executing will be finished after its Maximo due date on the current plan. Either go back to the Scope Challenge and raise the NC with its Maximo reference, or re-plan (crew, SSCE, sequence) so it finishes in time.

Before the scope freeze meeting

Click Scope Freeze Document and print it. You get the schedule layout, the full list of deferred PMs colour-coded by how close to due they are, and a signature block for Planner, Subsea Supervisor/OIM, Technical Superintendent and 3rd Party Verifier. Take it into the meeting and get it signed.

It also carries every Non-Conformity with its Maximo reference, the SSCE request status beside each change-out, and Certification due within the campaign: certified parts on the work being executed whose CoC falls due before the planned finish — the chance to recertify while the kit is already opened up.

5

Execute & Report

Where you'll live for the rest of the job. Set the Report Date first — almost everything on this page is calculated relative to it.

Recording progress

In Task & Milestone Actuals, for each task set a status (not started / in progress / complete), the actual dates, and a % complete. Then press Calculate Project.

Calculate Project — the button that does the work

Type all your percentages in first, then press it once. Anything at 100% becomes Complete with its actual finish set to the Report Date. Anything partial becomes In Progress and the tool re-forecasts every downstream date from the hours still remaining.

It works in one pass rather than recalculating on every keystroke, so nothing jumps around while you're typing.

Checklist — the job plan, done on site

Every task with a Maximo job plan has a ☑ Checklist button under its % box. It pops up the job plan as a checklist over the plan: mark each step Done / N/A / Not OK (N/A and Not OK need a comment), add photographs (several at once, or from an intrinsically safe camera later at a desktop), record materials used, and sign it off — Performed by and Verified by, Seadrill or third party. Close returns you to the plan.

The checklist's % drives the task's % Complete (Done and N/A count; each step is an equal share), shown read-only as from checklist. A task without a checklist keeps the typed %. Press Calculate Project afterwards, as always. A NOT OK step does not count and asks for a CM / Synergi reference; a signed checklist shows SIGNED ✔ · evidence linked.

While building the scope, clicking a job plan number still opens the read-only job plan with its NOV procedure links and parts. Steps ticked in REV 11 still count until a checklist is started.

Setting it up on your PC (once): run Scripts\Install-JobPlanChecklists.ps1, which copies Dan's checklist file from his pack to your local app-data folder (never OneDrive, because it carries the SACRED address), then restart the local server. If the file is missing, the pop-up says so and points you here. The pop-up always has its own ✕ Close bar — Esc or a click outside it closes it too — and Open in new tab if you prefer a full window.

Testing section and the 90% notice

A banner shows how far the Testing section is (Testing branch plus the Drift Test, by hours) and how many of the nine surface tests have their evidence. At 90% it says the pre-deployment call is to be arranged: teams review the Pre-deployment page and raise concerns ahead of the call. From REV 14 this goes to SACRED, which sends the Teams notice.

Actual Dur. Taken — fills itself in, type over it when you know better

The little label under the box tells you where the number came from:

  • forecast — nothing recorded yet, so it's showing the planned hours.
  • elapsed — worked out from your actual start and finish dates, for jobs spanning a day or more.
  • manual — you typed it. This always wins. Type 0 to hand the cell back to automatic.

Because the start and finish fields hold dates but no clock times, a job that starts and finishes on the same day can't be measured from them — so it shows the forecast hours instead of a misleading zero. Type the real figure over it.

Once you've typed real figures, the Report Cycle box above shows Recorded Hours vs Plan — counting only the rows you've actually filled in, so the comparison means something.

Milestones tick themselves. "LMRP Complete" goes green the moment every LMRP task is marked complete — you don't maintain them by hand. Type a date in yourself only if you want to override it, and your date always wins.

Above the actuals you get a timeline with the current critical-path item outlined in red, and a Next 24 Hours list — the day-to-day focus list for the rig.

Deferrals & Non-Conformities

Below the actuals, one table lists every work order deferred out of this campaign and every one raised as a Non-Conformity: WO number, job plan, location, discipline, the Maximo due date, the decision (deferred, or executing after its due date) and the Maximo NC reference. The same table prints in the Planning Report. A missing reference shows in red as MISSING.

The three logs

LogUse it for
Waiting Time (NPT)Hours lost waiting — permit, crane, weather, vendor, parts, rig ops. Log per day, per cause. Totals up by day and by category.
3rd PartyVendor personnel on board (with day rates) and project costs (freight, parts, rentals). Leave the off-board date blank for anyone still on board.
Break-in WorkUnplanned work that appeared mid-job. Auto-numbered BRK-001 onwards. Close each one out with a lesson learned when it's done.
NPT does not move your dates

That's deliberate, not a bug. Real delay already reaches the schedule through your task actuals and percentages — if NPT hours were added on top, the same delay would be counted twice and the finish date would drift too far out. The NPT log is there to report lost time, not to reschedule.

For the same reason: if a wait turned into an actual piece of work, raise it as Break-in Work instead of logging NPT for it — not both.

Pushing it out

  • Sync to Dashboard — saves the JSON to the shared reporting folder. This is the one that makes it appear on the dashboard.
  • Download Copy — same file, to your Downloads. Handy for emailing.
  • Print / Save as PDF — the human-readable Planning Report, including the NPT and 3rd party tables.
6

Pre-deployment

The pre-deployment checklist (FRM-00-0168 Rev 8) now lives here: Argo Navis holds the primary copy, and SSORT keeps a copy for emergencies only. Fill it in as the campaign goes, not on the day. Before the BOP is deployed, everyone signs it, like a flight checklist.

Every line has a state, shown by a coloured label:

  • Not started or In progress — still open.
  • Pre-filled (confirm) — the plan already knows the answer (break-ins, the timeline, SSCE requests). It never counts until its owner presses Confirm.
  • Complete or Evidence linked — closed.
  • N/A — closed only once a reason and a rig-management approver are both entered.

Some lines fill themselves in from the plan:

  • B-03 lists the work orders not completed, each needing its approved Maximo NC reference.
  • B-04 lists the MOC tasks.
  • B-19 follows the SSS-007 task.
  • The nine surface tests close when their evidence is linked: the SSORT record (FUNCTION, SOAK, DRAWDOWN, ACOUSTIC, ROV, EDS, EHBS) or an attached file (PRESSURE TEST charts, DRIFT record). ACOUSTIC is pre-filled N/A on rigs with no acoustic system.

Photographs go in labelled slots, FWD and AFT for every ram cavity. A slot can also point at the CBM post that already photographed it, as long as that photograph was taken after the last change-out on the cavity.

Signing

The checklist can be signed only when every line is closed, and never with less than SSORT's own minimum (designation, cavities, packers confirmed Yes, every photo slot). The open lines are listed at the top of the page.

Sign-offs go in order: TSL, then SS Supervisor, then Rig Manager. These are required. Operations Director, WCE Manager, Head of Well Control Equipment and Regional Superintendent are optional and never hold it up. The order shown is provisional until Dan confirms it.

The first signature fixes a fingerprint (SHA-256) of the whole checklist. If anything changes afterwards, a new revision starts and the signatures already given lapse; the earlier signed revision is kept. Sign-off is a typed name and date for now.

When the three required roles have signed, the page reads Pre-deployment complete. The pre-deployment call is a review, not a Q&A: it is recorded by SACRED's Help Centre, with these fields as a fallback.

Buttons:

  • Export (SSORT keys) — the checklist in SSORT's own format.
  • Designation — the BOP's required photograph slots, for SSORT and WCGRRT.
  • Print FRM-00-0168 — the printed form.

The bit that matters most

The weekly rhythm

You build the schedule once. Every report after that is a short visit to Step 5. Get this wrong and you'll cause yourself real problems, so it's worth reading twice.

WhenWhat you do
First time Steps 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5, then Sync.
Every time after Continue Existing Schedule (top of Step 1) → pick the JSON from your last Sync → you land in Step 5 → change the Report Date → update actuals → Calculate Project → Sync.

Two things lock permanently the moment you Sync for the first time, and both are on purpose:

  • The baseline. Your original finish date is captured and never moves again, so Plan Variance always measures against the plan you committed to — not against a target that quietly slides every week.
  • The filename. Fixed for the life of the project, so every Sync overwrites the same dashboard record instead of piling up duplicates.
Never rebuild from Step 1 for a follow-up report

If you start again from scratch instead of using Continue, you lose the locked baseline — so variance is measured from the wrong place — and everything already finished gets re-reported as "completed this cycle", because the tool has no memory of the last Sync. Use Continue.

Managing expectations

What it deliberately doesn't do

These are decisions, not gaps — so please don't report them as faults:

  • No P6 import. The instruction was explicit: this schedule is the single source of truth, so progress is entered here and nowhere else. Nothing is imported from another schedule to reconcile against.
  • Sequencing coverage varies by rig. Every rig with a Maximo site can be planned, but how much of its work the Branch Map can sequence differs — check the Sequenceable % in Step 1 before trusting a rig's schedule.
  • NPT doesn't drive dates. Covered above — it would double-count.
  • No full After Action Review register. Break-in items have an open/close lifecycle with a lesson-learned note, and that's the extent of it.
  • Steps don't re-sequence the schedule. Ticking job plan steps sets a task's % complete and records milestones; the schedule still plans at work-order level, and step dates are not fed back into the engine.
  • Nothing is written back. NC references, step ticks, certification lights and SSCE approvals are recorded or read here only — raise the NC in Maximo, approve the SSCE on its own dashboard, and update the CoC tracker in SACRED as normal.
  • Only PM work orders arrive in the feed. Corrective and breakdown work isn't in it, so anything unplanned still gets raised by hand as Break-in Work.

Anything on that list needs a conversation with Dan Plant before it changes.
