Named for the seam beneath our feet

Six hundred feet below Lambton Park runs the Five Quarter seam.

For two centuries the Great Northern Coalfield powered the world, and the Lambton Estate sat at its heart — the Lambton collieries sent their coal down the Wear to Sunderland and on to every port in Europe. Among the seams the pitmen worked, one name appears on colliery plans from Winlaton to Wearside: the Five Quarter, so called for its thickness of five quarter-yards of good coal. When Durham's mining engineers lettered the seams of the coalfield, the Five Quarter was seam G — sitting above the Main Coal, the Maudlin and the Low Main.

We've taken the name as our own — a fifth quarter, something extra beyond the expected — and we hope the pitmen would approve of what we do with it: honest food, generous measures, and a proper welcome, in the county that dug the seam.
